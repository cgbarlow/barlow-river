# LinkedIn post: The River

Final draft, 20 November 2026. The owner named the thrust: the danger of overspecification. There is a time to step out of the loop and a time a person has to be in it.

I gave my AI agents my family tree and one instruction: do something awesome with this. Then I went and made a cup of tea.

That is not how I normally work. Normally I write the spec. Then I write more spec.

What came back is the Barlow River. Four centuries of my family drawn as streams that join at each marriage. Norwich weavers, a mathematician at Woolwich, New Zealand by the 1850s. I would never have asked for a river. I'd have asked for a family tree, and I'd have got one.

That's the bitter lesson, as I read it. We pour everything we know into the instructions, with the best of intentions, and box in the very thing we hoped would surprise us.

It doesn't mean stay out for good. When the agents put the family on a globe, the view swung between Britain and New Zealand like a tennis match. It took a person to say stop that.

So write down what you know. My grandfather did, and it's the only reason there was anything to work with. Go easier on writing down what you want. And learn which moments need you in the room.

### The middle passage: three reworks

The Council's view (`.campaign/council-report-linkedin.md`): show the person who knows, do not define the idea. Use the talk's own lines. Recommended: A.

**A. Closest to the talk**

None of it works unless somebody wrote it down. Every family has a person who knows. Ours was my grandfather, and he had the decency to put it on paper.

Every organisation has that person too. Ask three people how something actually gets done and you'll get three answers. Most of it was never written down, and a fair bit of it is myth. Write it down properly and your agents can get to work on it. I've been calling that the organisation as code.

**B. Driest**

The agents got the credit. My grandfather did the work. He's the one who wrote it all down.

Most organisations have someone like him, except theirs hasn't written anything down and is on leave next week. That's the gap I've been poking at this year: the organisation as code. Get what the place knows out of people's heads and into something an agent can read.

**C. One paragraph**

The catch is that it only worked because my grandfather wrote it all down. Every family has a person who knows, and so does every organisation. The difference is that ours picked up a pen. That's what I mean by the organisation as code: write down how the place really works, and suddenly "do something awesome with this" is a fair thing to ask.

### Other storylines the data would carry

1. **The crossing.** Fourteen people in the tree are recorded on both sides of the world. All fourteen went out to New Zealand. Not one is recorded coming back.
2. **The mathematician.** A line of Norwich worsted weavers produces Peter Barlow, born 1776, who ends up teaching mathematics at Woolwich. His son patents the tunnelling shield used under the Thames.
3. **Twelve Peter Barlows.** One name handed down for three hundred years, and what it takes to tell them apart.
4. **Two families, one year.** A couple married in Lambeth in 1874 and had a child in Christchurch the same year.

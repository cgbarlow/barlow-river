# LinkedIn post: The River

Draft, 18 November 2026. Reviewed by the Council (`.campaign/council-report-linkedin.md`).

I gave my AI agents my family tree and one instruction: do something awesome with this.

Then I took my hands off the wheel.

The "bitter lesson" has been coming up a lot lately. Roughly: general methods and plenty of compute keep beating our carefully hand-built cleverness. I wanted to see what that looks like when the dataset is my own relatives.

What came back is the Barlow River. Four centuries of family drawn as streams that join at each marriage, with the shaky parts of the tree drawn as shaky.

It also counted. Twelve Peter Barlows. Several relatives marked as living who would now be well past a hundred. And only 14% of the people in the tree cite an actual record, which puts my years of careful research in a new light.

I still think there is no substitute for deep domain expertise, and the people who can bring it to agentic work will do very well. But Ethan Mollick talks about "conjuror" mode: point the agents in a direction and let go. Now and then it's worth it, if only to find out what you didn't know to ask.
